What is a VPN server, exactly?

A VPN server is a server belonging to a virtual private network. In short, it’s remote physical hardware used to route your connection when you use VPN software. When your VPN is active, your personal web traffic travels via an encrypted tunnel through a server before it is sent to its destination. This process takes place in secure tunnels. But increased security isn’t the only benefit. 

When you use a VPN, the server you connect to masks your true IP address. with its own IP address. As a result, you’ll appear as if you’re actually connecting from your server’s location rather than your true physical location.

With IPVanish, this process is further safeguarded by our RAM-only servers. Because these servers store no data at rest. Once a RAM-only server is powered down, all session data is instantly wiped, ensuring that your privacy is never left behind.

Why should I use a VPN server to change my IP address?

You may not realize it, but your IP address has significant ramifications over your online privacy and freedom. Without VPN protection, your real IP address is vulnerable to the prying eyes of third parties and snoopers. And with your IP address, they can target you with area-specific ads, monitor your personal internet activity, and even approximate your true physical location.

Additionally, due to overly-aggressive filters, some of the websites you visit may even wrongfully deny you access to certain pages, services, and media because of your IP address. Fortunately, however, you can change your IP address with IPVanish and eliminate these issues.

When you use a VPN server to change your IP address, your new IP address will match the server’s IP. This increases your online privacy, because it prevents snoopers from determining your true physical location. It also allows you to evade unjustified restrictions placed on your true IP address.

And with IPVanish RAM-only servers, that protection goes a step further. Not only is your true IP hidden, but your browsing data is never written to disk, reinforcing the reliability of our no-logs promise.

Will a VPN server improve my internet speed?

In most cases, a VPN server will not improve your internet speed. This is because when you use a VPN, your internet traffic is first rerouted before it reaches its final destination. Additionally, VPN use adds a layer of security to your connection process.

As a result, many VPNs will slow down your internet speed. Fortunately, with IPVanish, this speed difference is often undetectable. In fact, while you shouldn’t bank on a VPN improving your internet speed, there are some instances where it can. Most of these instances are related to bandwidth throttling, which occurs due to ISP tracking. When your internet service provider monitors your traffic and determines you’re using a lot of bandwidth, they can intentionally slow down, or throttle, your speed. 

Think of bandwidth throttling like a traffic jam. While under normal conditions one route may be the fastest, a traffic jam changes that. In those cases, another route could be faster, even if it’s technically a greater distance. For bandwidth throttling, a VPN works in a similar manner. A VPN server is more than likely a greater distance away than the servers your ISP uses to deliver your internet connection. But when they throttle that connection, rerouting your internet traffic may deliver a faster experience, regardless of the increased distance.

Even when bandwidth throttling is not a major issue, however, IPVanish still provides the fastest VPN experience possible. Our server network has been engineered for speed, with the most efficient hardware and software possible. Additionally, our VPN features have been specifically developed to ensure high-speed connections are always possible with IPVanish.

VPN Servers: Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does a VPN server do?

A VPN server acts as an intermediary between your device and the internet. When connected, your traffic is routed through a secure, encrypted tunnel to the VPN server, which masks your true IP address with the server’s IP. With IPVanish, this process is strengthened by RAM-only servers, ensuring nothing from your sessions is stored.

2. How do I get a VPN server?

To get a VPN server, you’ll need a few things. First things first, you must have internet access. You must also have access to a virtual private network via a VPN client. You can access the IPVanish network with an IPVanish subscription. Simply sign up for the IPVanish plan that suits your needs, then download the IPVanish app on your preferred device to get started.  

3. Which VPN server should I use?

Before you choose a server, you need to determine your purpose for using a VPN. If privacy and anonymity are your chief concerns, just connect to the country and city closest to your physical location. If your primary aim is to change your location, connect to the VPN server in the country or city you wish to appear in.

4. Are there free VPN servers?

Yes, there are VPN services that don’t charge to access their networks, but you should not use them. These free VPN services typically offer a limited number of servers to choose from, and often suffer from slow speeds and performance issues due to overloaded servers. And because they don’t cost any money to access, they have to make money in other ways. This could include selling user data to third parties, a major violation of privacy.

5. Is a VPN a proxy server?

No, a VPN is not a proxy server. A VPN does provide some of the same benefits of a proxy server, however, such as location-changing capabilities. If you’re looking for a proxy server, every IPVanish subscription includes access to a SOCKS5 proxy, a high-speed, more secure alternative to standard proxies.

6. What are RAM-only VPN servers?

RAM-only servers run entirely on volatile memory instead of hard drives. This means that once the server is powered off, all data is automatically wiped. With IPVanish, RAM-only servers ensure stronger privacy, faster updates, and a more secure infrastructure.

7. Do VPN servers keep logs?

Some VPN providers log and retain user data, but IPVanish does not. Our strict no-logs policy has been independently audited and verified, and with our RAM-only servers, there is no physical capability for your data to be stored.
